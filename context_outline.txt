---

## 7. Travel Logic

Travel should be calculated from a hidden base location controlled by the admin.

Rules:

* First 15 minutes of one-way drive time included
* After 15 minutes, charge based on **$50/hour**
* Travel fee should account for both directions
* Minimum travel fee: **$5**
* Round up to the nearest $1
* No hard maximum distance if the schedule allows enough time

Travel time must also affect scheduling. A 4-hour detail plus 1 hour of total travel should require a 5-hour availability window.

---

## 8. Payment Logic

Booking should require a deposit:

> **$50 or 25% of total price, whichever is greater.**

Customers may also optionally pay in full before the job.

Optional incentive:

> **Save 5% when you pay in full now.**

Remaining balance is paid after the job is completed.

# 9. Recommended Technical Direction

The current site is WordPress-based, but the requested functionality is closer to a modern custom booking application. The cleanest senior-level architecture is:

## Option A — WordPress-first implementation

Best for fastest launch.

Use:

* WordPress
* Custom theme
* Custom plugin for booking logic
* WooCommerce for payments
* Stripe for deposits
* Google Reviews plugin
* Booking/calendar plugin or custom calendar UI
* Google Maps API for travel estimates

## Option B — Headless / modern frontend

Best for maximum polish.

Use:

* WordPress as CMS
* Next.js frontend
* Three.js / React Three Fiber for interactive visuals
* Stripe for payments
* Google Maps API
* Custom booking engine
* WordPress REST API or custom backend

## My recommendation

Use a **hybrid approach**:

> WordPress stays as CMS/admin backend, but the booking experience becomes a custom modern front-end module embedded into WordPress.

This gives you:

* WordPress SEO and content control
* modern UI/UX
* better booking flexibility
* cleaner animation and Three.js support
* less plugin fighting

# 11. Three.js and Animation Strategy

Important constraint:

> Three.js should improve memorability, not slow down booking.

This is a local service website, so the booking flow must remain fast and clear. Three.js should be used as progressive enhancement, not as the foundation of the whole site.

## Best Three.js Uses

### 1. Homepage Hero Background

A subtle animated 3D car silhouette or glossy hood reflection.

Concept:

* Clean dark background
* Light reflections moving across a car surface
* Small spray mist particles
* Red accent lighting
* CTA remains fully readable

### 2. Service Package Interaction

When hovering over a package:

* subtle 3D tilt
* light reflection sweep
* small animated sparkle or clean-line effect

This can be CSS/Framer Motion rather than full Three.js.

### 3. Before/After Reveal

Use smooth image comparison sliders. Avoid heavy 3D here.

### 4. Loading / Brand Moment

A short spray bottle icon animation:

* spray particles
* car silhouette reveal
* logo fades in

Keep under 1.2 seconds.

---

## Animation Rules

Use:

* CSS transitions for basic movement
* Framer Motion or GSAP for UI animations
* Three.js only for hero or visual accent
* Respect `prefers-reduced-motion`
* Lazy-load heavy visuals
* Disable complex effects on low-end mobile devices

Avoid:

* 3D everywhere
* spinning cars
* distracting particles over text
* animations that delay booking
* heavy WebGL on the booking page

10. Travel Logic

Travel should be calculated from a hidden admin-set base location.

The customer should not see the base location.

Travel pricing rule

Let:

one_way_drive_minutes = estimated drive time from hidden base to customer
free_minutes = 15
rate = $50/hour

If one-way drive time is 15 minutes or less:

travel_fee = $0

If one-way drive time is over 15 minutes:

billable_round_trip_minutes = (one_way_drive_minutes - 15) × 2
raw_fee = billable_round_trip_minutes / 60 × 50
travel_fee = ceil(raw_fee)

Then:

minimum travel fee if charged: $5
round up to nearest whole dollar
no hard maximum distance as long as the full time fits in the schedule
Travel scheduling rule

Travel time should also count toward the schedule commitment.

Example:

4-hour detail + 30 minutes there + 30 minutes back = 5-hour calendar commitment
